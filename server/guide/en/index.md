# Guide

An exam goes through ten steps, in two groups. The sidebar lists them with their status: a tick when a step is done, a number when it needs you, and a lock once the papers are printed. You can open any step at any time.

## Before the exam

1. [Questions](guide:questions): write the questions and answers.
2. [Marking scheme](guide:marking-scheme): how answers are scored, the answer key, and the final mark.
3. [Papers](guide:papers): build the papers and check their layout.
4. [Print](guide:print): save the PDFs to print anywhere, or print directly on a Linux shared machine.

## After the exam

5. [Upload scans](guide:upload-scans): upload the scanned answer sheets.
6. [Check scans](guide:check-scans): settle what automatic reading couldn't.
7. [Mark](guide:mark): compute the marks.
8. [Match students](guide:match-students): link each sheet to a student in the class list.
9. [Export marks](guide:export-marks): get the marks out as CSV or ODS.
10. [Annotate](guide:annotate): make marked answer sheets to give back.

## Good to know

- **The preview panel** on the right shows the question paper, scanned pages, exported marks and annotated sheets. Drag its edge to resize it, or hide it with the button in the top bar.
- **Printing locks the exam.** After printing, the questions and layout must stay exactly as printed, or the scans can't be read. The marking scheme and the answer key can still change, in Marking scheme. Unlocking is possible, but removes scans and marks.
- **Out of date.** If you change the marking scheme or a scan after marking, Mark, Export marks and Annotate say so. Compute the marks again, then export and annotate again.
- **History.** Every save is kept as a version, for good: see what changed, compare, and restore the whole exam, one file or one change (Questions › History). Exam settings lists what was done and by whom.
- **Trash.** A deleted exam stays in the Trash on the Exams page for 30 days, with its history, and can be restored from there.
